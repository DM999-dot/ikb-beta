Football Pitch Markings

The Halfway Line: Separates the halves. You cannot be offside in your own half.
Penalty Area: Keeper hands allowed; fouls become penalties.
The "D" (Penalty Arc): Keeps players 10 yards away from the penalty taker.
Touchline & Goal Line: The boundaries. Ball is out when it fully crosses the line.
The 6-Yard Box: Where goal kicks are taken; keeps attackers away from keeper.
The Centre Spot: Where every half and every goal restarts from.
Academy Note: Every line on the pitch is considered part of the area it defines. For example, a ball on the line is still "in play".

Teams & Kits

The Traditional 1-11 Numbers

#1 Goalkeeper: The shot-stopper. Only one allowed hands.
#2 & #3 Full Backs: Defenders on the right and left boundaries.
#4 & #5 Centre Backs: The central defensive wall of the team.
#6 Holding Mid: Stays deep to protect the defence.
#8 Box-to-Box: Runs the whole pitch, attack and defence.
#10 Playmaker: The creative genius behind the striker.
#9 Striker: The main goalscorer and target man.
#7 & #11 Wingers: Creative attackers on the wide flanks.
Academy Pro Tip: Modern stars wear any number, but these core roles define the team's tactical structure.

Academy Pro Tip: The "Transformer" Systems

Goal Kick: Awarded when ball crosses goal line, touched last by attacker.
Penalty: A direct shot from 12 yards out for a foul inside the box.
Free Kick: Awarded for fouls outside the box. Direct or Indirect.
Corner Kick: Awarded when ball crosses goal line, touched last by defender.

1. The Penalty Kick

The Taker: Must wait for whistle; pauses are okay, feinting is not.
The Keeper: Must have one foot on or in line with the goal line.
Encroachment: No other players in the box or "D" until the kick.

2. The Free Kick

The 10-Yard Rule: Defensive wall must be at least 10 yards from the ball.
Attacker Proximity: Attackers must stay 1 yard away from a 3-man wall.
The Restart: Ball is in play as soon as it is kicked and moves.

3. The Corner Kick

Placement: Ball must be inside the arc or touching the white line.
Flag Integrity: Illegal to move or bend the flag to take the kick.
Direct Goals: You can score directly from a corner against the opponent.

4. The Throw-In

The Technique: Both feet on or behind line; ball thrown from over the head.
Field of Play: Ball in play once it enters. No direct goals allowed.
Opponent Distance: Defenders must stay 2 yards away from the thrower.
Academy Note: While these rules seem strict, they ensure the game restarts fairly and maintain match momentum!

The Offside Rule

The Golden Rule of Positioning

The Definition:
Head, body, or feet nearer to the goal line than the ball and the 2nd-to-last opponent.
The 'Active' Catch:
Only a foul if you interfere with play or gain an advantage from being there.
The Exceptions:
No offside directly from a Goal Kick, Corner Kick, or Throw-In.
Academy Note: Modern football uses VAR to check offsides using digital lines, often measuring by millimetres to ensure accuracy!

Modern Formations

THE 4-3-3 UNIT

Attacking Standard & Wide Strategy

Wide forwards play high and wide to stretch the defence, isolate full-backs, and create 1v1 chances.

TACTICAL RULE: THE SEESAW PRINCIPLE Tactics require balance. You cannot have everyone doing the same thing.
  • IF your Striker drops deep (False Nine)...
  • THEN your Wingers must run diagonally behind into the space he left.
THE PIVOT & THE HALF-SPACE The Half-Space is the vertical channel between the wide wing and the centre. Crucially, the Single Pivot (#6) sits deep to screen the defence. This security is what allows the two #8s to leave their post and push into these advanced channels to create a 5-man attack.
ELITE WINGER PROTOTYPE Modern scouts look for technical masters like Michael Olise (BAY) or Lamine Yamal (BAR). These players are prototypes because they possess the 1v1 dribbling and technical precision to exploit the space created by wide strategy.

The False Nine

Tactical Anomaly The 9.5 Role

A specialist striker who deliberately abandons the traditional center-forward position to drop deep into the midfield.

The Intelligence: By dropping deep, the False Nine forces opposing Centre-Backs into a lose-lose decision: follow the striker and leave a massive gap in the defensive line for wingers to exploit, or hold the line and allow the striker to turn and dictate play with a numerical overload in midfield.

THE 4-2-3-1 UNIT

The Five-Man Midfield Engine

The standard for balance: two anchors protect the defence while three creators operate between lines.

INVERTED FULL-BACKS Sometimes, a Full-Back will move inside to join the midfield in possession. This is called Inverting. The goal is to create a Numerical Overload in the center of the pitch.
DOUBLE PIVOT BALANCE In a 4-2-3-1, the anchors stay deeper to protect against transitions. If both pivots push too high simultaneously, it leaves the back four exposed to direct counter-attacks.
THE HYBRID LINE

The 3-4-3 Box Midfield

Modern elite systems use the 3-4-3 / 5-4-1 hybrid to control transition moments. This requires players (specifically Wing-Backs) to possess an "Elite Engine" - the stamina to cover the full pitch length for 90 minutes as they shift roles.

THE BOX MIDFIELD By moving two wide forwards narrower into the half-spaces, they join the two central midfielders to form a "Box". This creates a 4v3 or 4v2 numerical advantage in the centre of the pitch.
VERTICALITY VS U-SHAPE Elite systems prioritise Verticality (passing forward). If you fail to play vertical, you end up playing "U-Shaped Football"; harmless sideways passing around the opponent's block without ever penetrating.

The High Press

THE CONCEPT

Hunting the Ball

An aggressive defensive system where the team stays extremely high up the pitch to win the ball back near the opponent's goal.

UNIFIED PRESSING It starts from the front: the Striker acts as the first defender, triggering a "Unified Press" where the whole team moves as one unit. If one player goes and the others don't, the system fails.
THE 5-SECOND RULE Modern masters like Klopp and Guardiola instruct their teams to hunt the ball for 5 seconds immediately after losing it. If they don't win it back within this window, they retreat to a safer defensive block.
TACTICAL TRIGGERS

The Signal to Hunt

PRESSING TRIGGERS & SHADOWS A Pressing Trigger is a specific event, like a bad touch or a slow back-pass, that signals the team to hunt. During this, players use Shadow Cover, positioning their body to block the passing lane to a midfielder behind them while pressing the ball.
THE EXTRA DEFENDER

The Touchline "Cage"

The touchline is the only defender that never misses a tackle. By "shepherding" an opponent toward the side, the pressing team cuts passing options in half.

THE CAGE EFFECT Once an attacker is pinned against the white line, the touchline acts as a 12th man, trapping them in a "Dead Zone" with no escape routes.
RISK ASSESSMENT

The "Suicide" High Line

THE MANAGER'S DILEMMA

To press high, defenders must sit at the halfway line, creating a "Green Ocean" of space behind them.

  • High Risk: One accurate long ball creates a 1v1 with your keeper.
  • High Reward: You suffocate the opponent and win the ball near their goal.

Requirement: You cannot play this way if your defenders are slow. You need "Elite Recovery Pace" from Centre-Backs like Saliba (ARS) or Pacho (PSG) to track long balls.

MAN-TO-MAN DANGER Aggressive man-marking presses are high-risk; if a single player is bypassed, it creates a "Domino Effect" of open space that can lead directly to a goal.

Mid-Block & Transition

THE STRATEGY

The Mid-Block: The Spider Web

Unlike the High Press, a Mid-Block doesn't hunt the ball aggressively. The team sits in a compact shape around the centre circle, waiting for the opponent to make a mistake.

THE GOAL To congest the middle of the pitch. By denying space between the lines, you force the opponent to play "U-Shaped Football": Sideways passes around the block that never penetrate the centre.
DEFENSIVE ARCHITECTURE

The Low Block vs. Route 1

While some press high, others "Park the Bus". A Low Block involves defending very deep near your own penalty area to deny any space behind.

DEFENSIVE THEORY: Route 1 Masterclass

Route 1 Masterclass

Direct Football Press Bypass

The ultimate antidote to the High Press. Rather than risking possession near their own goal, teams kick the ball long, bypassing the midfield entirely.

The Intelligence: If an elite opponent commits 6 players forward to aggressively press you, a single accurate long ball completely removes them from the game. By aiming for a "High Outlet" player in the "Green Ocean", you flip the opponent's aggressive trap against them in one pass.

GAME MANAGEMENT: The Chaos Window

The Chaos Window

Transition Phase The 3-Second Rule

The most dangerous moment in football is the 3 seconds immediately after possession changes hands. Defences are expanded and utterly disorganized.

The Intelligence: Elite captains possess Transition Intelligence and must instantly read the game state to dictate the tempo:

  • Green Light (Chasing): Pass Vertical instantly. Exploit the chaos before the opponent can retreat into their defensive block.
  • Red Light (Winning): Put your foot on the ball. Kill the counter-attack, regain shape, and suffocate the opponent's momentum.
TRANSITION INTELLIGENCE Elite players possess Transition Intelligence: The ability to read the turnover before it happens. This allows them to start sprinting into space while the opponent is still reacting.

VERTICALITY IN TRANSITION

Elite teams use this window to pass Vertical (forward) instantly to catch the opponent while they are still expanded and disorganised.

Exploiting this window with a rapid, vertical offensive transition is commonly known in football as a Counter-Attack.

KYLIAN MBAPPÉ (THE HIGH OUTLET) Instead of retreating deep, he hangs on the shoulder of the last defender, ready to exploit the "Green Ocean" behind.

He acts as the "Release Valve": His job is to receive the long ball and hold it up or win a foul, allowing his teammates to breathe and move up the pitch.

Set Play Mastery

THE MODERN REALITY

The "Anti-Football" Myth

Elite clubs have realized that set plays are the most efficient way to break a deadlock. Modern giants like Arsenal now score nearly 30% of their goals from dead-ball situations.

DEAD BALL SITUATIONS

These routines are categorized as "Dead Balls" because open play has completely stopped, and the ball is totally stationary before the tactical restart begins.

OFFENSIVE ROUTINES

Launch Mechanics & Psychology

SET PIECE PSYCHOLOGY Elite routines use high-velocity trajectories and flat deliveries to "pin" defenders into their own 6-yard box. The Rory Delap Legacy showed that long throws act as "Virtual Corners", forcing defenders to face their own goal under extreme pressure.
ELITE RULE There is no offside directly from a throw-in. This Laws of the Game exemption is exactly why "Long Throws" are so dangerous. The strikers can stand behind the last defender legally.
THE DELIVERY

Inswingers vs. Outswingers

The curve of the ball dictates the panic. Analysts choose delivery based on the opponent's defensive height.

ELITE PHYSICS An Inswinger requires the ball to curve toward the goal. This specific trajectory is designed to increase the keeper's panic level and force an earlier reaction.

An Outswinger curves away from the goal. This forces the defence to move toward the ball while running toward their own goal, creating space for attackers to attack the ball at pace.
THE DEFENCE

Neutralising Modern Tactics

MARKING SYSTEMS To neutralise screening, elite teams have moved away from Pure Man-Marking (following one player) to Zonal Defending (guarding a specific area). Most now use a Hybrid System to cover both.

THE GOALIE SCREEN

Attackers use Screening to legally obstruct the keeper’s path. By delaying the keeper’s reaction, the offence gains a massive aerial advantage.

The Legal Boundary: Simply standing your ground is completely legal. Screening is only penalized as a foul if the attacker actively holds, pulls, or pushes the goalkeeper.

Total Football

DUTCH SPACE THEORY

Flexible Pitch Geometry

Founded by Rinus Michels and Johan Cruyff, Total Football suggests that the pitch is a flexible area. The goal is to expand space when attacking and contract it when defending.

POSITIONAL INTERCHANGE

This system requires Clockwork precision. If a defender pushes forward, a midfielder must drop to cover. This requires absolute precision and tactical intelligence to keep the shape constant.

This system relies heavily on Technical Centre-Backs who possess the playmaking ability to build attacks from the defence.

"Football is a game you play with your Brain" - Johan Cruyff

This claim remains the foundation of Total Football; technical skill is useless without the intelligence to manage space.
THE LINEAGE

Cruyff's Cathedral: The Guardiola Era

MOGUL RULE #2: THE JUNE 30 DEADLINE

The financial year ends on June 30th. If you are over the £105m loss limit on June 29th, you have 24 hours to save the club.

  • Buying is useless: Spending money increases your loss.
  • Selling a Star is risky: If their Book Value is high, selling them might create an Accounting Loss, making your deduction worse.
  • The Only Way Out: You must sell an Academy Graduate. Since their cost is £0, the entire fee counts as immediate profit, instantly wiping out your deficit.
CASE STUDY: NEWCASTLE UTD In 2024, Newcastle were forced to sell academy graduate Elliot Anderson to Nottingham Forest, and young talent Yankuba Minteh to Brighton. Because they had low/zero Book Value, their sales counted as 100% "Pure Profit", instantly saving the club from a points deduction.

Amortisation & Loopholes

ACCOUNTING TRICKS

The Accounting Engine

When a club signs a player for £100m on a 5-year contract they do not record a £100m loss immediately. Instead, the fee is spread evenly: £20m per year. This is Amortisation.

EXPLOIT

The Chelsea Loophole

How did Chelsea spend £1bn without breaking rules? They exploited contract length.

THE 8-YEAR CONTRACT By signing players to 8-year deals, they spread the cost thinner. A £100m fee became just £12.5m per year on the books. UEFA closed this in 2024, capping amortisation at 5 years maximum.

High Risk Accounting Loss

Amortisation protects you, until a player's performance collapses.

The Intelligence: If you sell him for £20m, you must record an immediate £16m Accounting Loss (£20m Sale - £36m Book Value). Because clubs can't afford this FFP hit, they are forced to keep deadwood players on the bench, creating a toxic "Wage Trap".

Player Power & Leverage (Click to Inspect)

CASE 01: The Bosman Ruling

The Birth of Player Power

EU Labor Law 1995 Precedent

Before 1995, clubs held absolute power. They retained a player’s registration even after their contract expired, meaning players couldn't leave without a transfer fee.

A player whose contract has completely expired and is without a club is known in the industry as a "Free Agent".

Jean-Marc Bosman sued his club, arguing this violated European Union labor laws (freedom of movement).

The Intelligence: The court ruled in his favor. Out-of-contract players can now leave for £0 (Free Transfer). This permanently shifted the power dynamic in global football from the clubs directly to the players and their agents.

CASE 02: The Kylian Mbappé Transfer

The Illusion of "Free Transfers"

Signing Bonuses Real Madrid (2024)

"Free Transfers" are never actually free. The money the buying club saves on a transfer fee simply goes into the player's pocket instead.

In 2024, Real Madrid signed Kylian Mbappé from PSG after his contract expired.

The Intelligence: Because there was no transfer fee paid to PSG, Mbappé's camp held all the leverage. They demanded a reported €100m+ Signing Bonus just for putting pen to paper. The cost to the club remains astronomical; it is simply categorized differently on the balance sheet.

STRATEGY

Mogul Logic: The 18-24 Month Rule

Elite clubs treat contract length as a ticking clock. Managing this timeline is the difference between a £100m sale and a £0 exit.

THE TIMELINE OF LEVERAGE:
  • 24 Months ("Amber Warning"): The last safe moment to renew. If they stall here, the asset value becomes unstable.
  • 18 Months ("Red Alert"): The "Danger Zone." Leverage shifts to the player. You must sell immediately or accept the loss.
The Golden Rule
"Renew at 24 months, Sell at 18 months. Never let it reach 12."
